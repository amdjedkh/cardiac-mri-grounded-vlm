## What is and is not new for us
CLAIM is the closest work: LeFusion-based generation, AHA-guided lesion placement, nnU-Net and EMIDEC. So "synthetic LGE scars with AHA-aware placement improve nnU-Net segmentation" is **not new** for us. This is a statement relative to the four papers and some search results, not a literature review; check before it goes in a paper.

What differs in our project (as far as I can tell):
- **The goal is grounded report generation, not segmentation.** Every Findings sentence is tagged to a region of the expert mask and checked against mask geometry; none of the four abstracts describes a per-claim geometry check against expert contours (not found in the abstracts, not proven absent).
- **Our synthesis is a different design.** Pretrained LeFusion is used as is (no segmentation model in its training loop); new shapes come from our own mask edits: real train lesions inserted into normal train hearts with random angle, width, depth and slice shift, with accept rules against a train-only reference.
- **Evaluation hygiene we document:** patient-level fixed split (80 train / 20 test), leakage checks on inputs and outputs, a fixed test set with no validation-fold peeking, a declared primary metric, repeated seeds and a patient-level bootstrap. CLAIM reports single numbers without variance [P].
- **Measured limits of the synthetic data** (contrast about 63% of real, 43 of 54 library lesions used, no seed; README.md) are stated next to the results.
- **A physician review tool** for images and reports.

Not new or not shown by us: large-scale pretraining, scanner integration, tool-calling, QA benchmarks, clinical validation, out-of-domain testing. Our results are on EMIDEC only, orientation is not yet cardiologist-confirmed, and the segmentation comparison has not been run.
